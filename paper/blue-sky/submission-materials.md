## Title

The Right to Dissent After Agreement: Persistent Challenges for Accountable Agent Decisions

## Abstract

An agent can change its answer without establishing that its earlier objection has been answered. We propose that agent teams distinguish current endorsement from the procedural standing of a registered challenge. A challenge would create an obligation to record a disposition before a decision is closed, even if its author later agrees with the majority. This vision connects generative-agent orchestration to public commitments and institutional protocol design. We specify a bounded challenge lifecycle, distinguish procedural safety from epistemic correctness, and identify the conditions under which persistent review can help or harm. A completed 600-question feasibility study motivates caution: across three repetitions, only one available panel had a correct minority against a wrong majority, and no protected review was activated. These observations establish neither benefit nor equivalence. We use them to motivate an evaluation agenda separating opportunity, activation, adjudication, and cost. The research challenge is to build auditable decision institutions that can preserve unresolved objections without giving agents an unlimited veto.

## Rationale for Blue Sky — draft visible to reviewers

This submission proposes a research agenda for accountable closure in agent institutions: a registered objection can create an obligation that survives changes in the agent's current endorsement. The envisioned impact extends from short LLM debates to persistent multiagent workflows, where evidence, responsibilities, and unresolved challenges must survive agent replacement and context loss. The paper provides explicit lifecycle semantics, bounded procedural guarantees, counterexamples to accuracy guarantees, and an evaluation agenda that distinguishes evidence retention from authority and review allocation. Its pilot is reported as a calibration failure, not an efficacy result. The central claim is a proposed direction for agent institutions, whose practical value is deliberately left open to falsification. Author review should strengthen the connections to normative-agent systems and demonstrate why the direction extends beyond existing memory and adjudication mechanisms.
